# Detailed Guide to Scrumban Meetings and Ceremonies

## 1. **Daily Stand-up**

- **Purpose**: Align team members on current work status, surface impediments, and clarify daily goals.
- **Procedure**:

  - Hold daily at a fixed time (typically in the morning) for no more than 15 minutes.
  - Conduct the meeting standing up so it stays brief and focused.
  - Each team member briefly shares answers to the following three questions:

    - What did I complete yesterday?
    - What do I plan to work on today?
    - Are there any blockers or impediments in my current work?

  - Hold the meeting in front of the Kanban board to visually inspect work progress.

## 2. **Backlog Refinement**

- **Purpose**: Continuously review, reprioritize, and break down backlog items into actionable work units.
- **Procedure**:

  - Hold periodically (e.g., 1 to 2 times per week).
  - The Product Owner (PO) and relevant stakeholders participate to adjust story priorities.
  - Analyze items with the team and split tasks into smaller units when necessary.
  - Improve shared understanding and readiness so upcoming work proceeds smoothly.

## 3. **Release Planning**

- **Purpose**: Establish medium- to long-term goals and formulate feature release schedules.
- **Procedure**:

  - Conduct on demand or on a regular cadence (e.g., monthly or quarterly).
  - The entire team collaborates to agree on target features and release timelines.
  - Combine the Kanban board with Scrum's sprint concepts to structure the plan.
  - Set realistic schedules by accounting for Work in Progress (WIP) limits.

## 4. **Review Meeting**

- **Purpose**: Share completed deliverables and gather feedback.
- **Procedure**:

  - Hold periodically or as needed.
  - Team members demonstrate completed work or share outcomes.
  - Stakeholders and the PO attend to provide feedback.
  - Discuss areas for improvement alongside successful practices.

## 5. **Retrospective**

- **Purpose**: Reflect on the team's workflow and collaboration practices to identify improvements.
- **Procedure**:

  - Hold on a regular cadence (e.g., every two weeks or once a month).
  - All team members participate.
  - Typical agenda:

    - Share what went well
    - Identify problems and blockers
    - Brainstorm improvement ideas
    - Select actionable items

  - Incorporate the selected action items into the next cycle for execution.

## 6. **Prioritization and WIP Limit Management Meeting**

- **Purpose**: Inspect WIP limits—a core pillar of Kanban—and adjust priorities to optimize workflow.
- **Procedure**:

  - Conduct as needed whenever the situation requires.
  - Check whether the team is currently exceeding its WIP limits.
  - Identify bottlenecks or delays and discuss solutions.
  - Reprioritize items when new work is introduced.

---

# Additional Notes

- **Leverage the Kanban Board**: Run all meetings around the Kanban board (physical or digital) so work visualization remains central.
- **Flexible Execution**: Because Scrumban has no rigid sprints, meeting frequency and content can be adjusted as needed.
- **Importance of WIP Limits**: Strictly adhering to WIP limits is essential for improving efficiency and minimizing bottlenecks.
