# How to Run a Daily Scrum

1. **Fixed Time & Place**

- Same time every day (e.g., 9:00 AM)
- Same place (meeting room or online video call)

2. **Participants**

- Entire development team (developers, QA, etc.)
- Scrum Master (facilitator); Product Owner (PO) may attend as an observer

3. **Format**

- Timeboxed to **15 minutes or less**
- Team members **take turns sharing**
- Answer **3 core questions** briefly:

  1. What did you do yesterday?
  2. What will you do today?
  3. Are there any blockers impeding your progress?

4. **Core Principles**

- Focus on **high-level alignment** rather than deep technical explanations
- Discuss blockers separately after the Scrum ends
- Aim for **team synchronization**, not status reporting

# Daily Scrum Sample Script

## Overview

- **Time**: Every morning at 10:00 AM (15 minutes max)
- **Attendees**: Entire development team, Scrum Master, and Product Owner (PO) if needed
- **Format**: Standing meeting (video call for remote teams)

---

## 1. Opening & Purpose Alignment (2 min)

**Scrum Master**:

> "Good morning, everyone. Let's start our Daily Scrum. Please share what you did yesterday, what you plan to work on today, and any blockers you're running into."

---

## 2. Team Progress Updates (10 min)

### Alex (Developer)

> "Yesterday, I completed the user login API. Today, I'm going to implement the user profile lookup feature. No blockers at the moment."

### Sam (Designer)

> "Yesterday, I finished the payment page UI design and handed it off to the development team. Today, I'll start designing the admin page. However, there are a few points I need to align on with the dev team—could we have a quick sync this afternoon?"

**Scrum Master**:

> "Sure, I'll coordinate a time with the relevant team members right after this meeting."

### Jordan (Backend)

> "Yesterday, I worked on database optimization, and today I'll run performance tests. However, CPU utilization on the test server looks high, so we may need to monitor it."

**Scrum Master**:

> "I'll log that as a blocker and follow up with the infrastructure team after the meeting."

### Casey (QA)

> "Yesterday, I finished testing the login feature, and today I'll start testing the newly added payment feature. There are a few test scenarios I need the dev team to confirm."

**Scrum Master**:

> "I'll connect you with the developers involved as soon as we wrap up."

---

## 3. Blocker Summary & Action Plan (2 min)

**Scrum Master**:

> "To recap the blockers shared today:
>
> 1. Design–dev alignment meeting needed
> 2. High CPU utilization on the test server
> 3. QA test scenario confirmation needed
>
> I'll coordinate with the respective owners on these three items."

---

## 4. Closing (1 min)

**Scrum Master**:

> "Everyone is aligned on today's work and we've captured all blockers. Let's wrap up here, and those who need to follow up can stay on for separate discussions. Have a great day!"

---

## Tips for Running an Effective Daily Scrum

- **Keep it timeboxed**: Standing during the meeting is recommended to stay within the 15-minute limit
- **No problem-solving during the standup**: Schedule separate time for deep-dive discussions
- **Encourage full participation**: Foster an environment where every team member speaks evenly
- **Use visual aids**: Pair the standup with a Kanban board or task management tool
- **Focus on blockers**: Always establish concrete action plans to remove impediments

# Daily Scrum Facilitation Checklist

## Facilitator (Scrum Master) Checklist

- [ ] Did the meeting start at the same time and place (or online link) every day?
- [ ] Are you prepared to wrap up within 15 minutes?
- [ ] Did you guide each team member to share only **Yesterday / Today / Blockers**?
- [ ] Were all reported **blocking issues** recorded for separate follow-up?
- [ ] After the Scrum, did you pull in only the people needed for follow-up issue resolution?

---

## Team Member Sharing Checklist

- [ ] What did I complete yesterday?
- [ ] What will I work on today?
- [ ] Is anything blocking my progress?

Keep updates **brief and clear (1–2 sentences)**.
Save problem-solving discussions for after the Scrum.

---

## Team Member Update Template

```
[Name]
- Yesterday:
- Today:
- Blockers:
```

Following this structure lets team members speak **directly from the checklist** every day, while the Scrum Master simply **updates the table** to handle both documentation and team alignment at once.
